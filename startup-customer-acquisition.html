Customer Acquisition  /  5 Min Read

Choose a path
you can repeat.

The best early channel is not the one with the biggest promise. It is the one that helps you learn how the right customers can find and choose you.

Published 13 October 2026Crescita
In this pieceStart with the customerChoose a laneLearn before scaleMeasure customer quality

A startup can be present in many channels and still have no reliable way to acquire customers. The team posts, advertises, experiments, and attends events—yet each month starts without knowing which effort deserves to happen again.

The way out is not to find a universally “best” channel. It is to choose a plausible path to the people who need the product, then learn whether that path can work for this business.

Begin with the customer’s decision.

Write down who is most likely to experience value soon, what they are trying to accomplish, and where they already look for help. A channel only matters if it can reach that customer in a context where the offer makes sense.

Talk to recent customers and people who considered the product but did not choose it. Ask what prompted the search, what alternatives they compared, what nearly stopped them, and what evidence made the decision easier. Their answers help distinguish an accessible audience from a convenient assumption.

Choose a lane the team can learn from.

Early on, focus is useful because it makes cause and effect easier to see. Choose one primary acquisition path based on customer fit, time to feedback, team capability, and cost to reach a meaningful test. A founder may start with direct outreach or partnerships; another product may have a natural search, community, or referral path. The right choice depends on the buying behaviour, not a trend list.

  • Define the audience narrowly. Name the customer group and moment of need the test is meant to reach.
  • Make the offer specific. State the value and the action you want the customer to take.
  • Set a learning window. Decide what evidence is enough to continue, revise, or stop.

First Round Review’s acquisition playbook argues for identifying a startup’s most promising route to market instead of diffusing scarce attention across every available channel. Read its customer acquisition playbook for a deeper discussion of channel choice.

Use hands-on work to reduce uncertainty.

At the beginning, manual effort can reveal what a scaled process would need to reproduce. Speak directly with prospects, personally onboard early customers, or help them complete the first task. Notice where interest fades, which questions repeat, and what creates confidence.

This work is not a permanent substitute for a repeatable system. It is a way to learn what the system should do. Y Combinator’s startup advice describes the value of staying close to early customers and understanding what is needed before trying to scale.

Measure what happens after the click.

Reach and response tell only part of the story. Follow the path from first contact through qualified demand, activation, repeat use, and contribution. The precise measures will differ by business, but the principle is stable: judge the route by the customers it creates and the value they retain, not by a low-cost impression alone.

Keep the test small enough to interpret. Record the audience, offer, activity, cost, customer behaviour, and what you learned. If the signal is weak, ask whether the audience, message, or path was wrong before declaring the whole channel ineffective.

A repeatable acquisition path is built through a sequence of informed choices. Start close to the customer, make one route legible, and let what happens after acquisition shape the next investment.
